To the Editor-in-Chief
Journal of Information Security and Applications

Dear Editor,

We are submitting the manuscript "Layered Zero-Knowledge Verifiable Artifacts for
Offline Deep Q-Network Training over Committed Replay Data" for consideration as
a research article in the Journal of Information Security and Applications.

The paper addresses a practical assurance problem: when a decision-making model
is trained offline on a fixed dataset, an auditor who is shown only the final
model has no way to check that the training actually consumed the data it was
supposed to consume. We build and measure a working system that produces
zero-knowledge proofs for this setting, and we report what it costs.

We would like to be explicit about what kind of submission this is, because the
manuscript contains formal statements and we do not want them to be mistaken
for its contribution. This is an implementation-and-measurement paper. The system
comprises six proof relations implemented as SP1 zkVM guests in Rust, each
mirrored by an independent Python oracle so that a specification disagreement is
caught before a prover runs; 315 automated tests; and 26 proof configurations
measured end to end on real hardware, including GPU proving runs, rather than
estimated analytically. Twenty-five of the 26 configurations verify in
0.054-0.130 s with proofs of 1.27-2.85 MB. A tamper benchmark exercises 236
adversarial cases across 19 categories and yields no unexpected acceptance. The
largest result places an entire 4,992-step training run under a single recursive
proof. The ten security statements in the paper exist to document precisely what
each measured configuration guarantees, and each is mapped to the relation code,
the backend, the tests, and the benchmark rows that support it.

Two findings came out of building the system rather than reasoning about it, and
we report both. A fixed-point overflow was accepted by the guest and caught only
because the Python oracle uses arbitrary-precision integers; the resulting design
publishes the admitted value range as part of the public statement. Separately,
deriving the replay sampler seed from the dataset commitment closes a freedom
that a valid proof does not otherwise constrain.

The complete artifact, including source code, dataset commitments, SP1 proof
provenance records, the self-collected replay datasets, and the scripts that
regenerate every reported table, is publicly archived at
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.XXXXXXX. All reported numbers can be reproduced
from it.

We state the scope limits plainly in the paper: the system does not prove a
complete DQN training history from initialization, does not cover the Adam
optimizer, and its recursive aggregation requires a CUDA prover. We report the
cost of provability in policy quality rather than omitting it.

To our knowledge no reinforcement-learning system appears in the existing
zero-knowledge verifiable machine learning literature, which has so far
addressed supervised training.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under
consideration for publication elsewhere. There are no competing interests to
declare. Use of generative AI in preparing the manuscript is declared in the
paper.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

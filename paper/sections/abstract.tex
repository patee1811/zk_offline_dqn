\begin{abstract}
A recent survey of zero-knowledge verifiable machine learning scopes itself to
supervised learning, and the training systems it covers are for regression,
support vector machines, $K$-means, decision trees and deep networks; no
reinforcement-learning system appears. Value-based RL is not simply a larger
computation: it carries three verification obligations supervised constructions
never meet. Its labels are produced by the model being trained, so a proof must
cover how each label was derived; target-network synchronisation is a discrete
event a prover can move while keeping the run internally consistent; and replay
sampling has no canonical order, so a prover who picks the draw picks the data
the claim is about. We present a
layered artifact system that gives zero-knowledge proof-backed verification of
offline Deep Q-Network (DQN) training over provenance-bound committed replay
data. Datasets are committed with SHA-256 Merkle trees after a replay audit.
Relations are stated in fixed-point integer arithmetic and implemented twice,
as a Python oracle and as an SP1 zkVM guest, so that a disagreement between
them is caught before a prover runs. The fragment relation derives its sampler
seed from the dataset commitment rather than accepting one, publishes a bound
on the values it will admit, and clips gradients component-wise.

Aggregation comes in two modes. A chunk chain binds child proof manifests for
$T \in \{32, 64, 128\}$; recursive aggregation verifies each child proof
cryptographically inside the guest, which we measure for $T \in \{16, 32, 64\}$
flat and for binary trees up to $T = 4{,}992$. The largest of these is a single
root proof covering an entire $4{,}992$-step run, bound to the dataset that
produced a policy we report.

We state and sketch proofs for ten security statements, each mapped to relation
code, backend, tests and benchmark rows. On CartPole and LunarLander, with
PointMaze from D4RL backing the dataset-scaling rows, twenty-four of the
twenty-five proved configurations verify in $0.054$--$0.197$\,s with proofs of
$1.27$--$4.31$\,MB; the exception verifies Groth16 child proofs in-guest and
costs $68$\,s and $1.47$\,GB. A tamper benchmark of $236$ adversarial cases
across $19$ categories yields no unexpectedly accepted row. We also report what
the provable configuration costs in policy quality rather than leaving it to
the reader: on \texttt{lunarlander-random} it reaches $-152.7$ against $-215.3$
for the tuned minibatch baseline, and an ablation isolates the target-sync
interval -- not the minibatch, and not the clipping rule the proof forces -- as
what governs whether it learns.

The contribution is a deliberately scoped
security/systems artifact: it does not claim complete DQN training soundness
or Adam optimizer proofs, and its recursive aggregation requires a CUDA prover
and is demonstrated up to $T = 64$.
\end{abstract}

The system has five roles.  A dataset collector or importer creates replay
data and metadata.  A prover opens committed transitions and model/checkpoint
witnesses and produces relation proofs or semantic-verifier evidence.  A
verifier checks public statements, proof public values, and artifact hashes.  An
auditor/report generator checks source artifacts and emits Tables 1--3.  A
benchmark runner records proof cost and tamper-rejection evidence without
changing the relations.

\subsection{Assets and Public Inputs}

The protected assets are replay dataset contents, dataset commitments, manifest
and audit reports, model weights, checkpoint hashes, private witness values,
proof artifacts, and benchmark tables.  The verifier may see the dataset root
or Merkle root, manifest hash, audit-report hash, raw trajectory hash,
collection-log hash when available, configuration hash, input/output
checkpoint hashes, relation-specific public outputs, proof verification key
material or public values, and published benchmark metrics.  These values are
public by construction.

The private witness boundary is relation-specific.  Dataset transition values
may be private witnesses to membership and training relations, but the dataset
may also be public or committed elsewhere.  Merkle paths are witnesses unless a
report discloses them.  Model weights and intermediate tensors can be private
when only checkpoint hashes are public.  Checkpoint hashes, dataset roots, and
public report tables are not private.

\subsection{Adversary Capabilities}

The malicious prover may try to alter a transition reward, action, done flag,
or next state; use a transition outside the committed replay set; tamper with a
Merkle path, leaf index, or root; change a deterministic minibatch index;
change selected Q values, TD targets, losses, gradients, or weight updates;
skip or reorder checkpoint steps; tamper with target-network synchronization;
reorder aggregation chunks; swap child proof manifests; alter child public
input hashes; or verify a proof against different public inputs.  The verifier
accepts only if the implemented relation and public-input binding checks pass.

\subsection{Reward and Dataset Correctness Boundary}

For self-collected datasets, the audit pipeline can replay the environment and
check reward, next-state, termination, truncation, and collection-log evidence
before commitment.  After commitment, proofs and verifiers check consistency
with the committed and audited data.  They do not establish reward correctness
for arbitrary external datasets beyond the import/source-integrity checks that
were actually performed.  Public Minari/D4RL benchmark rows are
source-integrity benchmark imports, not honest collection proofs.

\subsection{Non-goals}

The artifact does not claim a full DQN training proof, Adam optimizer proof,
all-replay-batches proof, model-selection proof, no true recursive aggregation,
honest public dataset collection proof, or optimality of the learned policy.
It also does not provide confidentiality for values intentionally published in
fixtures, reports, tables, public inputs, or proof metrics.
